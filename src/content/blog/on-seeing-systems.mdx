It is very easy to get caught up in the noise. Every day brings another product, another vulnerability, another headline, another promise to make security faster, smarter, or more efficient. Keeping up has become a full-time job.

But are we getting better?

Not better at processing alerts. Not better at adding controls. Not better at doing the same things faster.

Better at security.

We spend so much time improving the processes we inherited that we rarely stop to ask why those processes exist in the first place. Why do we think the way we think? Why do we use the tools we use? 
Why do we interface with them the way we do? What problem were they originally meant to solve?

We are often too busy operating the machinery to question the machinery. We know when something feels wrong. We know there must be a better way. But we are so consumed by the work in front of us that we rarely have time to question the systems, assumptions, and processes that shaped it.

There are better ways. But to move forward, we may need to look backward: to understand how we got here, why these systems exist, and which assumptions we have stopped questioning. If we want to improve as an industry, we need to return to the fundamentals.

The more I return to the fundamentals, the more room there is to think differently. By stepping outside the assumptions, tools, and processes we have inherited, we can ask better questions about the systems we are trying to protect and perhaps find better ways to protect them.

Those systems are always changing. Adapting. Evolving. We may never see them completely, fully understand them, or be able to control them. But we can learn to see them more clearly.

If we want to become better defenders, we cannot just get better at doing the same things.

We have to learn to think differently.
